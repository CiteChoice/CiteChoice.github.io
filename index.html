When several retrieved pages support the same fact, an answer engine cites some and skips others. CiteChoice audits that choice causally: it freezes real search transcripts from a tool-using agent, changes one thing about one source, and regenerates the answer.

Still open

Does structure change whether a source is cited at all? The pre-specified estimate is +4.5 percentage points, but the interval includes zero (p = .168). Two simple mechanisms, list markers on their own and a general rank effect, also failed repeat or held-out checks.

The question

Who gets the citation?

Answer engines retrieve pages, write an answer, and attach citations to its claims. Most evaluation asks whether those citations are correct: does the cited page actually support the sentence?

This study asks something that only comes up when several retrieved pages support the same claim. Citing either page would be correct. So which one gets the credit, and what moves that choice?

We call this citation allocation. A citation is a unit of visibility in generative search, and publishers already tune pages for answer engines. Knowing which document properties move citations causally, and by how much, matters for science and for the web's information economy.

Observational data can't answer it. Pages shown first are also more relevant, better written and chosen by the agent's own queries. CiteChoice holds all of that fixed by replaying the same frozen transcript and changing one cue at a time.

What the answer model actually reads

Retrieval returns serialized page text, not rendered pages. Visual layout, typography and the DOM are gone. Headings, list markers and table rows survive. That serialized text is the layer CiteChoice manipulates, so the treatments vary a cue that is already everywhere in real model context.

The implication for Document AI: extractor and serializer choices decide which structure reaches the model, and that boundary can have downstream consequences for citation credit.

Replay lab

Run the 2×2 yourself

Every pair was replayed four times: two renderings of the target crossed with two orders of the pair inside their original search call. Pick an example and flip the switches. Counts and excerpts are the archived GPT-5.4 outputs for that exact cell.

The three named examples were picked because something visibly moves. Most pairs look calmer: at the family level, 61 of 89 showed no change in whether the target was cited. Use Random pair, or the Explorer, to see typical cases.

Findings

Finding 1 Supported

Structured rendering concentrates citation credit

+0.50target citations per answer, 95% CI +0.20 to +0.84, Holm p = .033

Rewrite the target with headings, short paragraphs and lists, keep every fact and roughly the same length, and the answer cites it more. Raw means rise from 2.69 target citations per answer under prose to 3.12 under structured rendering, a 16% increase.

The family-weighted causal estimate is +0.50 citations. It is one of only two of 16 declared secondary tests that survive Holm correction. Distinct answer sentences citing the target rise by the same +0.50, so this is not one sentence collecting repeated markers.

The rest of the answer barely moves. Structured answers carry 0.49 fewer citation markers in total, 0.05 fewer unique sources and 42 fewer characters, none reliably different from zero. The matched competitor changes by −0.02. The credit is redistributed toward the target, not added.

The magnitude repeats under fresh decoding of 30 frozen families: +0.54 in the first generation, +0.55 in a new one, +0.55 averaged (p = .013). That is independent decoding of the same transcripts, not a new dataset.

Is it real? Resample it yourself.

The same two procedures the paper uses, run live on the 89 real family effects.

Resample the families with replacement and recompute the mean, 20,000 times. The middle 95% of those means is the interval.

Sign-flip test · p-value

If structure did nothing, each family's effect would be equally likely to point either way. Flip signs at random 200,000 times and count how often the mean is as extreme as the real one.

Cited at all? Not settled.

The single primary hypothesis, declared before scaled outcomes existed, was about incidence: is the target cited at least once? The estimate is +4.5 percentage points (95% CI −1.4 to +10.4; p = .168). Eighteen families favor structured rendering, 10 favor prose, and 61 don't change.

That is directional but inconclusive. With the observed variance, 89 families give about 80% power only for incidence effects of roughly 8.5 points or more. Restricted to the 103 human-confirmed pairs, the estimate is +5.9 points (p = .066). Record-disjoint, cross-model and fresh-decoding checks all point the same way; none turns it into a confirmed admission effect.

Why can the count be clear while incidence is not? 75 of the 113 pairs cite the target in all four cells or in none, so incidence has no room to move there, while the count can shift in any answer that cites the target. Switch the stats lab to “Cited at all” to watch the null distribution swallow the observed value.

Where the extra credit comes from

A post-hoc blinded LLM audit matched all 1,313 target citations to the pair's verified shared facts. About half attach to evidence the competitor also carried; the other half to content only the target offered.

48.6% shared
50.7% target-specific

Structured rendering adds +0.25 shared-evidence citations (p = .023), half the total effect, and raises the chance of any shared-evidence citation by 8.7 points (p = .0005). A second audit finds the answer expresses the shared facts about as often either way (+0.8 points) while the target's credit for them rises 3.6 points (p = .042). The competitor loses nothing; point estimates place the offset among other sources (−0.97 markers, p = .058).

Both audits are post hoc and LLM-scored, and p-values here are uncorrected. They describe visible credit, not whether the model internally relied on a source.

Finding 2 Supported

The observed rank gradient overstates the controlled effect

5×the observational gap is five times the scaled controlled estimate, and has no held-out counterpart

In the acquisition corpus, documents first shown at rank 1 were cited 85.1% of the time; at rank 5, 42.8%. That 42-point gap mixes position with everything that travels with it. Search engines put more relevant, higher-quality pages first, and the agent chose the queries that produced those rankings.

To isolate position, CiteChoice swaps the two members of a matched pair inside their original call and changes nothing else. Order reaches the model only through array position; there is no rank or score field. Placing the target above its competitor raises incidence by +7.9 points (95% CI +1.1 to +14.9; Holm p = .350). In 56 held-out pairs swapped on their original text, the effect is exactly 0.0 (95% CI −5.4 to +5.4).

The observed and controlled numbers come from different populations, text regimes and channels. Treat the chart as a comparison of scale, not a decomposition.

What position can and can't do

Wider swaps moved more in the scaled replay, up to +30 points for the ten full-window swaps from slot 1 to slot 5. The held-out pairs cover comparable one-to-four-slot swaps and stay near zero in every stratum, including nine full-window cases.

The supported conclusion is narrow: message position can move attribution in some frozen transcripts, especially for wide swaps, but average rank effects read off observational gradients are not reliable. This extends the “smaller than it looks” pattern from realistic RAG answer quality to citation allocation.

Finding 3 Measured

One generated answer is a noisy measurement

15%of cited-or-not cells flip when the same answer is regenerated

A single generated answer is a sample from a distribution. CiteChoice regenerated all four cells for 30 hash-chosen families from byte-identical inputs and compared the two decodings cell by cell.

Exact family effects agree for only 17 of 30 families, even though the aggregate count effect repeats almost exactly. Aggregates are stable; single examples are not.

A method-of-moments decomposition attributes 45% of single-draw family-effect variance to decoding noise; the rest is real heterogeneity between families. Averaging k generations per cell shrinks the noise part by about 1/k.

At the study's design (89 families, one generation per cell) the paper reports about 80% power only for effects near 8.5 points. At the same variance, 4.5 points would need roughly (8.5 / 4.5)² × 89 ≈ 318 families. The sliders rescale the 8.5-point figure with the paper's variance split (decoding noise .050 of .112 total). This is arithmetic on reported numbers, not a reported result.

For evaluators: repeat cells, report agreement and between-generation variance, and keep aggregate repeatability separate from per-example stability.

Boundaries Unconfirmed mechanisms

Simple recipes don't survive the checks

The structured and prose renderings are both rewrites. They share the same facts, but not identical wording (Jaccard similarity .80; 36% of structured sentences appear verbatim in the prose). So the main contrast is a rewrite package, not pure formatting.

To isolate formatting, a post-hoc mechanical ablation took the prose arm's exact word sequence and laid it out one sentence per list row. No model was involved and no word changed. Try it on the passport target.

Over all 113 pairs this raised incidence by +6.7 points (95% CI +1.1 to +12.4; p = .028). On the 30 repeatability families it reversed: −1.7 originally, −8.3 fresh, −5.0 averaged (p = .22). Serialization can matter, but the reversal rules out a stable list-marker mechanism.

post hoc diagnostic

List markers alone

Positive in the full cohort, reversed in the repeated subset.

secondary · confirmation

Moving a source up

The rank-by-structure interaction is −4.5 points (p = .418), and the held-out average rank effect is zero. Adding lists or moving one source upward doesn't reliably win citations across transcripts.

No stable one-variable recipe.

cross-model

Another answer model

Replaying the same frozen GPT transcripts with Grok 4.3 points the same way: +5.6 points after protocol repair, bounded at +2.7 to +7.1 under worst-case completion. But only 44.7% of Grok's first responses met the citation contract, the unrepaired estimate is +0.9, and GPT–Grok cell agreement is 69.9%.

Direction holds across one more model; magnitude doesn't transfer.

An earlier, record-disjoint 100-query wave is positive in aggregate, but families that recur across waves correlate negatively (r = −.445, resting on only four families that moved in both). Local effects belong to a specific frozen transcript, not to the underlying fact.

Implications

Presentation is part of the attribution system

For citation evaluators

Don't trust a single draw

Fifteen percent of binary cells flip under identical regeneration, and decoding contributes 45% of single-draw effect variance. Repeat cells, report agreement and between-generation variance, and separate aggregate repeatability from per-example stability.

For auditors and publishers

An audit warning, not a growth hack

Presentation can move visible credit even when matched sources carry the same evidence. The supported effect is concentration, not a confirmed rise in being cited at all. The competitor isn't suppressed, and no stable list-marker or rank recipe emerges.

For Document AI pipelines

Version the serializer

Extraction doesn't erase structure. Headings, lists and table rows are pervasive in the text handed to the answer model, so extractor and serializer choices belong in the attribution pipeline's audit trail, not in neutral preprocessing.

What remains open

The pre-specified incidence effect sits below the study's power resolution, the pure-serialization diagnostic is unstable, and the rank estimate fails held-out confirmation. What moves here is visible citation credit, not established causal reliance on evidence.

A confirmatory next wave would

  • pre-specify count and incidence as co-primary outcomes
  • repeat every cell instead of drawing one generation
  • acquire fresh trajectories under a harmonized one-shot citation contract
  • vary retrievers and answer models
Protocol

Under the hood

The estimand

Each pair contributes four trials, one per cell, so every effect is a difference taken inside a single pair. For pair i, let Y = 1 if the target is cited at least once. The structure effect averages over the two rank assignments:

Δistruct = ½(YiSH + YiSL) − ½(YiPH + YiPL)

S and P are structured and prose; H and L put the target higher or lower than its competitor. The rank effect averages over text arms the same way. Pair effects are averaged within answer-target families, and all estimands are defined over the 89 families.

The target is the expected effect over decoding randomness, conditional on the frozen transcripts, the cohort and the answer-engine configuration. Because the whole answer is regenerated, each effect is end to end: uptake, phrasing and sentence boundaries can move along with source choice.

Inference and guarantees

  • Confidence intervals from a 20,000-replicate family bootstrap; two-sided p-values from a sign-flip permutation test, exact up to 20 discordant families and 200,000 seeded flips beyond.
  • One primary hypothesis declared before scaled outcomes existed; 16 secondary tests in one Holm family; everything else labeled exploratory, supplementary or post hoc.
  • Replay rebuilds the provider-native conversation with live search disabled and deterministic opaque source handles, so identifiers can't leak the original order.
  • Before any model call, an integrity gate diffs each manipulated transcript against baseline and fails closed on any undeclared change. All 452 trials passed and archive pre- and post-intervention hashes.
  • Zero live searches, zero citation repairs, zero missing cells in the scaled GPT run. 31 transient transport failures across 25 pairs were retried under unchanged trial definitions.
  • Acquisition and primary replay: GPT-5.4 at temperature 0.2, one sampled generation per cell. Evidence review, treatment generation and audits: Grok 4.3.

Scope

Limitations

External validity

One retrieval provider (Exa, five results per call, bounded text), one acquisition agent and primary answer deployment (GPT-5.4), and the transcripts it produced. Frozen replay can't show whether a publisher-side edit would change retrieval, later search turns or live ranking.

Power and multiplicity

89 independent families and only 28 discordant families on the primary incidence outcome. The approximate 80% minimum detectable incidence effect is 8.5 points, so +4.5 is unresolved rather than evidence of zero. The count result is one of 16 declared secondary tests.

Construct and treatment

The primary contrast is a jointly generated rewrite package with lexical differences. No scaled cell uses the untouched original target text, and both arms compress it symmetrically. The ablation isolates one serialization form only. The strict citation contract (median 29 markers per answer) may not transfer to sparse-citation systems.

Mechanism and post-hoc analysis

The word-preserving estimate flips sign in the repeated subset, displacement strata are exploratory, and the alignment and expression analyses are post hoc and LLM-scored. Regenerating the whole answer can mix uptake, wording and attachment changes.

LLMs in the measurement loop

Grok 4.3 generates treatments and, in a separate blinded pass, gates their fidelity: procedural separation, not an independent model family. The 23-item treatment spot audit was assistant-led. Shared-evidence labels were LLM-produced with exact-quote checks, then human-adjudicated for every analyzed pair.

Data and reproducibility

The archive keeps search-returned snapshots, not full webpages. Licensed third-party text and raw provider traces can't be redistributed automatically, so the artifact releases code, prompts, hashes, derived records and no-network analysis regeneration.

Ethics: queries are synthetic and manually revised; no private user data or human participants are involved. Manipulations happen offline in replay and never touch live pages or search indexes. Because the findings could encourage presentation-only optimization, effects are reported with their uncertainty as audit diagnostics, not optimization guidance.
